# Landing Page Art Direction

## Establish the conversion story

A strong landing page answers:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why does it matter now?
4. How does it work?
5. Why should I trust it?
6. What should I do next?

The visual order may differ, but the answers must be discoverable.

## First viewport

The first viewport needs:
- recognizable category or context;
- specific promise;
- primary action;
- one strong evidence or demonstration device.

Avoid vague headlines such as “Reimagine your workflow.” Prefer concrete outcomes.

The hero does not need to be centered. Consider:
- editorial split with a live product strip;
- full-bleed product canvas with anchored copy;
- typographic manifesto paired with evidence;
- sequential story opening;
- interactive before/after;
- asymmetric media crop;
- compact high-density launch board.

## Section selection

Use only sections justified by content:
- mechanism;
- outcome;
- proof;
- use cases;
- comparison;
- integration;
- objection handling;
- pricing;
- FAQ;
- final action.

Do not include every common landing-page section.

## CTA discipline

- One primary action label across the page when possible.
- Secondary actions should not visually compete.
- Make the next step explicit.
- Repeat the CTA after meaningful proof, not at arbitrary intervals.
- On mobile, consider sticky action only when it improves usability.

## Proof

Use evidence in descending strength:
- actual product interaction;
- measured outcomes with source;
- named case study;
- specific testimonial;
- customer logos with permission;
- process detail;
- credible guarantees or policies.

Never fabricate proof.

## Build quality

- Render meaningful content without JavaScript when the stack allows.
- Ensure metadata, canonical URLs, social preview, and structured data where appropriate.
- Optimize above-the-fold media.
- Keep the first interaction responsive.
- Validate conversion controls and forms.
